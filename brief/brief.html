Monsoon Twin

A fast, physics-aware Climate Digital Twin of India — and a second universe for every shock.

Multiverse of Monsoon Team CHACHA Code & weights: github.com/choudharyjayesh668/monsoon-twin Live demo: monsoon-twin-production.up.railway.app

01USP

Turn the last three days of India's atmosphere into a 10-day, branching forecast in about 0.1 s on four CPU cores — no GPU, no network, no external weather service. A shock added to the last day becomes a second universe: the dashboard shows where it starts, how it spreads, and what it changes.

  • Fast enough to branch: 10-day rollout in 0.09–0.14 s; 3.1 s cold start; one container serves the twin and the demo.
  • Causal by construction: 3×3 convolutions only; a local shock cannot teleport across India in one step.
  • Judge-ready: training code, weights, dashboard and a 2-page brief in one offline repo; validator and repo scan pass.

One step = one day. The state is the anomaly from a 366-day climatology, divided by a per-channel scale. The network predicts the change of that anomaly; the next state is the last state plus that change, re-anchored to climatology. Rain is clamped at zero inside the loop and at the wire. The server is separate from the dashboard and only imports twin.py.

03Model design

  • State: 6 physical fields — t2m (K), tp (mm/day), u10/v10 (m/s), msl (Pa), tcwv (kg/m²) — on 32×36 cells, 3 days, oldest first.
  • Network: 773k weights; 3×3 stem, five residual 3×3 blocks, 1×1 head. No pooling, attention, downsampling or map-wide normalisation. Reach: 6 cells/day.
  • Training: score-matched loss — half pooled squared error scaled by the better reference, half per-window RMSE over its own better reference. 5,000 one-step + 3,000 four-step iterations; MLX bfloat16, ~18 min.
  • Split: train 2005–17, tune 2018–19, report 2020–21; ship the same recipe on 2005–21.
  • Heavy rain: emitted rain lifted above 10 mm/day where expected rain passes a per-lead cutoff (8.5 → 6.25 mm). Emitted only; fed-back state is never lifted.

04How drift and blur are prevented

  • Drift: predict the change of the anomaly, add it to the last state, then re-anchor to climatology. Rain ≥ 0; output guards (rain ≥ 0, −40…+60 °C, finite). 100% valid for 14 steps.
  • Determinism: no dropout, no sampling. A zero shock is bit-identical to no shock.
  • Blur: local residual updates, no pooling/attention, no ensemble mean. Day-8 spatial variance 0.73–1.01× the truth across all six fields; 10/10 no-blur points.
  • Training signal: four-day unrolled rollouts teach the twin how far to relax toward climatology from its own output.

05Failure modes we know about

  1. Day 8 is close to climatology. Holdout skill is −0.112; the twin relaxes well but does not predict new weather at that range.
  2. Extreme rain is under-dispersed. Peaks are smoothed; the 10 mm/day lift trades misses for false alarms. The most extreme 2020–21 heatwave and winter windows can lose to the better baseline.
  3. Shock limits. Weaker Arabian wind fades before spreading in ~46% of windows; +15% Bay moisture can push single cells past 5σ in ~26%.
  4. Leap years. No year is sent; from March the climatology slot is one day late (~−0.004 skill at days 4–8).
  5. Domain edge. Inflow outside 32×36 is invisible; edge forecasts degrade first.
  6. Distribution shift. Hidden 2023–24 is outside 2005–21; demo windows are in-sample.

Who uses it. District and relief teams triage which districts to inspect; agriculture advisors flag wet spells; planners branch a warmer Bay or a weaker monsoon and watch the alert area move. It is a screening demo, not an operational warning or evacuation system.

03 · Evidence & limits

  • Validator: all checks passed on local, Docker and Railway deployments.
  • Repo scan: 15/15, 0 flags; training code and weights inside the repo.
  • Offline: CPU only, 4 cores, 6 GB, no external APIs; dashboard separate from the server.
  • Stability: 100% valid for 14 steps; null shock bit-identical.

Honest limits

  • 1° daily grid. Local extremes are not resolvable; exact rain-peak location is uncertain.
  • Day 8 ≈ climatology. Extreme rain peaks are underpredicted and heavy-rain false alarms grow; event-window variance is the main scoring risk.
  • In-sample demo. The demo windows are 2020–21, seen during final training; hidden 2023–24 is out-of-sample.
  • Flood alerts are a screening aid, not evacuation guidance.
